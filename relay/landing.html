sigil relay_

It carries your approvals. It cannot read one.

Think of it as a coat check that only takes locked boxes. Your Mac and your phone share a key this relay never gets. Every message is locked shut before it arrives, and only the far end can open it. Even the name tags are scrambled, so the relay cannot tell whose box it is holding. It hands boxes back and forth all day and holds a key to none of them.

That is why it is safe to run wide open. No accounts, no passwords, nothing to trust on the path your secret takes, because there is nothing on that path worth stealing. The safety is in the lock, not the server.

What it carries

  • The first hello. When your Mac and phone pair, they trade sealed introductions so each learns the other's key.
  • A request to approve. Something on your Mac reached for a secret and wants a yes before it hands one over.
  • Your answer. Allow or deny, sealed on your phone with a fingerprint or a face.
  • A doorbell address. Your phone leaves a sealed note so it can be buzzed the moment a request is waiting.
  • A "this one is settled" nudge. When a request is answered or times out, the other side hears it is done.coming

Every message it moves is one of these, and every one is a locked box. The relay routes each to the right coat-check hook and passes it on, still locked. It has never opened one and has no way to.

Why this exists

It is two-factor authentication for your command line. Every secret on your machine is one careless command, or one over-eager AI agent you handed the keyboard, away from walking out the door.

Sigil puts a gate in front. When something reaches for a secret, your phone lights up first: allow, or deny. "It can read every secret, always" becomes "it can ask, and you decide, one tap at a time."

Do not trust us. Read the code. The whole design assumes you will.
